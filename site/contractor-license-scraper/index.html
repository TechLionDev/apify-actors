Contractor License Scraper: New Contractor Leads WA OR TX NY

Licensed contractors from official state data (Washington, Oregon, Texas, New York): phones, addresses, trades, license dates, bonds. New-license leads.

Run it on ApifyAll 23 TechLion Dev Actors$1.00 / 1,000 licenses

A first test run is usually under a cent. No login to the source site. Pay only for the results you get. Built and maintained by TechLion Dev.

Contractor License Scraper collects licensed contractors from official state license data in Washington, Oregon, Texas and New York into one clean table: business name, phone number, street address, city, ZIP code, trade, license number, license status, start and expiration dates, owner or principal, and (in Oregon) bond and insurance details. Pick the states, optionally a trade such as roofing, HVAC or electrical, and press Start.

It is built for new-contractor leads. Every week hundreds of businesses get a new contractor license. They need insurance, bonds, trucks, tools, suppliers, accounting, software, websites and marketing, and they are easy to reach because the license lists a phone number and address. Insurance agents, building suppliers, software companies, marketing agencies and AI (artificial intelligence) agents use this data to find them first.

The data comes straight from each state's public open data portal, published by the state licensing agency. There is no scraping of websites, no login and no proxy, so runs are fast, cheap and stable: 200 licenses take about 6 seconds.

  • New-license leads: contractors licensed in the last 7, 30 or 60 days, newest first.
  • Trade lists: every licensed roofer, plumber, HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning) or electrical contractor in a city, ZIP code or county.
  • Renewal reminders: licenses expiring in the next 30, 60 or 90 days.
  • Contractor checks: confirm a license number, status, bond and insurance before you hire.
  • Weekly feed: schedule the Actor and get only contractors you have not seen before.

What data does it extract?
🏢 Business name ☎️ Business phone number
📍 Street address, city, state, ZIP, county 🔨 License type, trade and specialty codes
🪪 License number and status 📅 Start date, days since licensed, expiration date, days left
👤 Owner, principal or responsible managing individual 🏛️ Business type and state business ID (Washington)
🛡️ Bond company and amount (Oregon) 📄 Insurance company, amount and expiration (Oregon)
👷 Workers' compensation, minority or woman owned, debarred (New York) 🗺️ Source state and licensing agency

Which states and licenses are covered?

State Source What is in it
Washington (WA) Department of Labor & Industries Every registered construction contractor (general and specialty, about 160,000 records including expired)
Oregon (OR) Construction Contractors Board Every active contractor license, with bond and insurance
Texas (TX) Department of Licensing and Regulation Air conditioning, electrical, sign, appliance installation, elevator and water well contractor licenses (you can choose other license types)
New York (NY) Department of Labor Contractor Registry Contractors registered for public works jobs

Texas data has no license start date, so Texas rows are sorted by license number (highest first, which is usually newest) and the Licensed since filter does not apply to Texas.

Why this scraper?

  • 4 states in one Actor and one table. Other contractor license actors cover one state each.
  • Cheaper: $1.00 per 1,000 licenses. Single-state license actors charge $4.00 to $19.00 per 1,000.
  • Official data only. Every row comes from a state agency's own published dataset.
  • New-license and expiring-license filters built in, plus city, ZIP, county, trade and phone filters. All filters are free.
  • Plain-English trades. Type HVAC and the Actor also matches each state's own wording (A/C, heating, refrigeration).
  • New-lead tracking for scheduled runs.

Expiring within (days) Only licenses that expire soon.

Only licenses not seen on earlier runs Save only new licenses on scheduled runs.

The dataset has three views: Overview, Lead list (contact columns for a CRM, or customer relationship management, import) and Compliance (license, bond and insurance dates). A RUN_SUMMARY record lists, for each state, how many rows were checked and saved, plus the filters used.

How to use it

  1. Click Try for free and sign in to Apify (a free plan is enough to start).
  2. Choose one or more States.
  3. Optional: add a trade (roofing), a city or ZIP code, and Licensed since (30 days) for new-contractor leads.
  4. Click Start. When the run finishes, open the Output tab and download Excel, CSV (comma-separated values) or JSON (JavaScript Object Notation).
  5. For a weekly lead feed, turn on Only licenses not seen on earlier runs and add a schedule.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find newly licensed contractors?

Set Licensed since to a date or a relative value such as 30 days. Washington uses the start of the current license period (renewals reset it), Oregon the first registration date, and New York the registration date. Rows come newest first.

Where does the data come from?

From each state's official open data portal, published by the licensing agency: Washington Department of Labor & Industries, Oregon Construction Contractors Board, Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, and the New York State Department of Labor. The Actor reads the public data feeds those portals provide. The data is as fresh as the state publishes it; in November 2026 Washington, Oregon and New York updated daily and Texas weekly.

Does it include emails?

No. State license lists include phone numbers and addresses but not emails. To add emails, run a website contact details scraper on the business websites.

Why are there no roofers in Texas?

Texas does not license roofing or general contractors at the state level. The Texas data covers air conditioning, electrical, sign, appliance, elevator and water well contractors. Use Texas license types to choose other Texas license types.

Will you add more states?

Yes, as long as the state publishes its contractor license list as open data. Open the Issues tab and tell us which state you need.

Is it legal to use this data?

The data is public records published by state agencies for anyone to use. Some rows name owners or principals, so follow your local marketing and privacy laws and do-not-call rules before contacting businesses. If you are unsure, ask a lawyer.
